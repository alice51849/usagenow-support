Why you only notice the limit once it's too late

AI coding services meter usage in windows. Claude has a 5-hour session limit and weekly limits, including per-model weekly caps. When you run Claude Code on a Pro or Max plan, that's the meter you're burning through. ChatGPT Codex also works with a 5-hour window and a weekly window. GitHub Copilot counts premium requests, chat and code completions, and Gemini has daily request quotas for each model.

All of these numbers live somewhere in each service's settings, which is exactly where you aren't looking while you work. You're in a terminal, an editor, a pull request on a second monitor. And the reset doesn't land on a neat round hour: a 5-hour window ends at whatever time five hours after you started happens to be, which nobody remembers. Add a second account, say one for work and one for personal projects, and you lose track twice as fast.

The fix isn't another browser tab you'll forget to check. It's a glance at the phone that's already sitting next to your keyboard.

Set it up in five steps

  1. Download UsageNow from the App Store and open it. There's no UsageNow account to create.
  2. Add your first service, for example Claude. You sign in on that service's own login page, and your sign-in stays in your device's Keychain.
  3. Add any other accounts you use: work and personal, Pro and Max, Codex, Copilot or Gemini, all side by side.
  4. Put a widget on your Home Screen (long-press an empty spot, tap the add button, choose UsageNow) and, if you like, another one on your Lock Screen. If you wear an Apple Watch, add the complication to your watch face.
  5. Create an alert: choose the 5-hour limit, the weekly limit or all limits, then type the percentage that matters to you, like 90 or 98. Add as many alerts as you like.

Each alert fires once per limit and cycle, so your Lock Screen stays quiet instead of nagging you every few minutes. Turn on the reset alert as well and you'll know the moment a limit is back, so you can pick up the session you had to pause.

Choosing alert percentages that actually help

An alert is only useful if it gives you enough room to act. A few setups that work well for day-to-day agent work:

Everyday habits that save you from the wall

  • Glance at the widget before a long run. If the 5-hour window resets in twenty minutes, it's often smarter to wait than to let an agent get cut off halfway through.
  • Don't forget the weekly limit. The 5-hour window comes back quickly; the weekly one doesn't. A dedicated weekly alert keeps you from a surprise late in the week.
  • Sort by the next reset. With more than one account, sort by 5-hour reset or weekly reset and switch to whichever account frees up first.
  • Use the Lock Screen. A Lock Screen widget means you see where you stand every time you pick up your phone, without opening anything.
  • Use your Apple Watch in meetings. The complication shows your status without putting your phone on the table.
