The multi-account problem

With a single account you might get by checking a settings page now and then. With several, that falls apart. Which account still has room in its 5-hour window? Which one is close to its weekly limit? When does the work account free up again? Answering that means signing in to several dashboards, doing math on reset times, and usually guessing wrong.

The cost shows up at the worst time: an agent run stops halfway, you switch accounts, and that one is nearly out too. What you actually want is one screen that shows every account's remaining usage and reset time, ordered so the right choice is obvious.

Sorting by 5-hour or weekly reset

Sorting is where multiple accounts become manageable. UsageNow lets you sort by:

  • 5-hour reset: the account whose session window frees up soonest comes first. Handy when you're about to start a long agent run.
  • Weekly reset: useful late in the week, when the weekly limit is the one that matters.
  • Highest or lowest usage: see at a glance which account has the most room left, or which one to rest.
  • Name or service: keep Claude, Codex, Copilot and Gemini grouped together.
  • Your own order: drag accounts into whatever order matches how you work.

A simple routine: when an alert tells you one account is getting close, open the list sorted by usage, pick the account with the most room, and keep going.

A widget that flips through every account

You don't need to open the app to check another account. You can flip through all of your accounts right on the widget, and a tap refreshes it whenever you want the latest numbers. There are Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets in every size, and Apple Watch complications for checking without your phone. UsageNow runs on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.

Alerts work across accounts too. Choose the 5-hour limit, the weekly limit or all limits, then type the percentage that matters to you, like 90 or 98, and add as many as you like. Each alert comes once per limit and cycle, so your Lock Screen stays quiet, and a reset alert tells you the moment a limit is back.

Adding more accounts doesn't mean handing your credentials to anyone new. You sign in on each service's own login page, your sign-in stays in your device's Keychain, and UsageNow talks only to the services themselves. There is no UsageNow server, no account to create and no tracking.

Do you need an app for this at all?

Honestly, not always. Every service shows your usage somewhere in its own settings or usage page, and that costs nothing. If you use one account, rarely hit a limit, and don't mind checking a dashboard now and then, you're fine without UsageNow.
