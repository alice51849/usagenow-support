What a Copilot usage tracker should show

For GitHub Copilot, UsageNow shows three things: premium requests, chat and code completions. Each one comes with a live countdown to its reset and the exact reset time written the way your region writes dates and times, so there is no mental arithmetic about when things free up again.

Copilot figureWhat you see in UsageNow
Premium requestsHow much you have used, plus a countdown to the reset
ChatCurrent usage and when it resets
Code completionsCurrent usage and when it resets

Copilot alone decides what its limits are and when they reset; UsageNow simply displays those values so you do not have to go looking for them.

Copilot at a glance on your Home Screen

The whole point is not having to open anything. UsageNow Pro gives you Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets in every size, plus Apple Watch complications. Tap a widget to refresh it whenever you want the latest figures, and flip through all of your accounts straight from the widget. UsageNow runs on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.

Alerts before you run short

Make your own alerts: choose the limits you care about, then type the percentage that matters to you, like 90 or 98. Add as many as you like. Each alert comes once per limit and cycle, so your Lock Screen stays quiet, and a reset alert tells you the moment a limit is back.

A sensible routine for Copilot: set one alert early enough to change how you work (saving premium requests for the harder problems), and one late alert as a final warning.

Copilot next to Claude, Codex and Gemini

Most developers who track Copilot also use something else. UsageNow keeps them side by side:

  • Claude: the 5-hour session and weekly limits, including per-model weekly caps
  • ChatGPT Codex: the 5-hour and weekly windows
  • GitHub Copilot: premium requests, chat and code completions
  • Gemini: daily request quotas for each model

Work and personal accounts sit side by side too. Sort by 5-hour reset, weekly reset, highest or lowest usage, name or service, or drag accounts into whatever order suits you.

You sign in on each service's own login page, your sign-in stays in your device's Keychain, and UsageNow talks only to the services themselves. There's no UsageNow server, no account to create and no tracking.

Do you need an app for this at all?

Not always. GitHub shows your Copilot usage on its own settings pages, and that costs nothing.
